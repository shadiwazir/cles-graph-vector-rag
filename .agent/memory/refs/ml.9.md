# ml.9 Improvement plan: overconfidence and what could raise the results, 2026-11-16

- Trigger: after the 16-bit serving check (docs/05) the user asked what would improve the results and how to overcome the overconfidence. The answer was documented as docs/08; no step started.
- Measured basis: fine-tuned 4-bit "high" top routes 17, right 8 (47%); 16-bit 50%; wrong "high" 9 then 7; majority baseline 42%; top route 46% (4-bit), 50% (16-bit), base 62%, OpenAI 50%; 24 late-stage examples, error about +-20 points.
- Checked in the dataset (23afd5bb, train.jsonl, 359 examples): first-route likelihood is high 143, medium 204, low 12. The first route always equals the real outcome (by construction of the target), and the likelihood is written by the target LLM that knows the outcome, then capped by evidence (`cap_likelihood`, training/target.py). So the model is taught to lead with a confident route.
- Two separate problems: (a) overconfidence, a target-design problem that is fixable; (b) outcome accuracy, which probably stays near 50% with this data volume; the honest goal is calibration, not accuracy.
- Why serving-time calibration first: free, deterministic, no GPU, replayable on the stored outputs; it shows how far calibration alone gets. Caveat: a rule fitted to 48 answers overfits; derive it from a principle (precedents agree on the outcome) and validate on unseen examples.
- Why computed likelihood in targets: the number should come from code (outcomes of retrieved similar decisions), the model explains the route; this removes the leak "the target writer knew the answer".
- Why more evaluation data before trusting gates: a 2/3 gate on a handful of "high" answers cannot separate a gain from luck (Wilson interval is wide).
- Retrieval: citations were verified but not always relevant in the 2026-11-15 console test (a sexual-assault sentencing decision quoted for a statement-admissibility case); the verifier checks that a quote exists, not that it fits.
- Honest limit: no promise that any step reaches the 67% gate; if not, record that the serving-time calibration is required.
- Related: [ml.7] evaluation, [ml.8] acceptance, ADR 0017, ADR 0021.
- 2026-11-16 (second part): user raised the heterogeneity of legal data and asked how to build training and evaluation sets for it; decision: measure honestly first (ADR 0022, docs/08 step 0, done).
- Findings from the data (107 decisions, split 2026-10-07): eval is 12 decisions x 4 stages (so the real n is 12, the earlier +-20 points was too optimistic); outcome prior shift (train 74/93 dismissed = 79%, eval 5/12 = 42%, distance 0.37); eval mixes criminal (8) and civil (4, including extradition-like and mental-health matters). Precedent vote (plurality of the context decisions' outcomes) is right 14/24 = 58% on late-stage examples, always-dismissed 10/24 = 42%, fine-tuned 46-50%, base 62%, OpenAI 50%; all intervals over 12 decisions overlap. When the precedents are unanimous (8 examples) the vote is right 5/8 = 62%, better than the model's own "high" (47-50%).
- Family detection: panel + date is useless (a panel decides many unrelated appeals in a week, e.g. 2026 ONCA 715 and 721). Shared party names work but initials (D.W., C.D., J.W., R.G.) name different people; real families exist inside train (D.W. 2023 -> 2024 retrial appeal) but none cross the split; one false candidate came from a regex that dropped digits ("2206747 Ontario Limited"), fixed.
- Why the decision as the unit and cluster bootstrap: the four stage examples of one decision share facts and outcome, so counting them as independent overstates the evidence; with 12 clusters the percentile interval is wide (about +-25 to 30 points) and that is the honest width.
- Why baselines in the same tables: a fine-tune is only worth its cost on outcomes if it beats what retrieval alone says; the precedent vote is also the natural anchor for "high" (step 1).
- Built: evaluation/statistics.py, evaluation/baselines.py, training/diagnostics.py, family_* fields in the pack training.yaml, decision fields in scoring._summary, baselines in eval_score (report, metrics/eval.json, gauges, MLflow runs), acceptance criterion outcome_vs_precedent (tracked), check_dataset warnings. 193 tests pass.
- Not built (docs/08 steps 1-4): the calibration replay, target rebuild, rolling folds with locked test, issue-type taxonomy, retrieval changes. Open question for the user: how many more PDFs, and from which years/courts, to widen the evaluation.
